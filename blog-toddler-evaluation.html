5 signs your toddler may need an evaluation

Children 4 min read

Every child develops at their own pace — but a few specific signs are worth a professional look rather than a "wait and see."

Parents hear "every child is different" so often that it can start to feel like permission to ignore real red flags. Most of the time, a little unevenness in development is nothing to worry about. But a handful of specific signs are worth acting on early, because early intervention tends to work faster and more completely than support that starts later.

1. Few or no words by 18 months

Most toddlers have a small but growing vocabulary by 18 months — even if it's just a dozen or so words. A toddler with almost no spoken words at this age, especially alongside limited pointing or gesturing, is worth having assessed.

2. No two-word combinations by 24 months

Phrases like "more juice" or "daddy go" typically show up by age two. If a child is still relying entirely on single words at 24 months, that's a pattern worth checking rather than waiting out.

3. Speech that's hard for family to understand

Some unclear speech is completely normal for toddlers. But if close family members regularly can't understand what a two- or three-year-old is trying to say, it's a reasonable reason to get an opinion.

4. Loss of previously used words or skills

Gaining and losing a few words here and there is common. Losing a broader set of words or social skills that were previously in place is different, and always worth a prompt evaluation.

5. Limited interest in back-and-forth interaction

Babbling back at a parent, responding to their name, or showing interest in shared attention (like following a pointed finger) are early building blocks of communication. A toddler who consistently seems uninterested in these exchanges may benefit from a closer look.

What an evaluation actually involves

An evaluation is low-pressure — usually a mix of play-based observation and simple structured tasks, plus a conversation with the parent about what's been noticed at home. It results in a clear picture of where a child currently stands, and a plan if support is recommended. Many toddlers evaluated early never need more than a short course of therapy.
